It's been almost a year since I started working as an SRE at SnappPay, and during this time, I've learned a lot about what SRE really means in practice.

Our task is not just to implement SRE practices, but to build the foundation and culture for SRE in a fast-growing company. Along the way, I've realized that this is a much bigger problem than I initially thought.

### Reliability Is More Than Technical Stuff

Before this journey, I mostly saw SRE as an engineering discipline: infrastructure, monitoring, metrics, and all the technical work needed to keep services reliable.

Of course, these things matter. A solid technical foundation is one of the most important parts of the job.

> **Technical practices alone cannot make an entire product reliable.**

Keeping a whole product reliable is not just about the health of individual services. It's also about:

- their dependencies
- how teams work together
- how problems are communicated
- everything around the systems that can affect them

The more I worked on SRE, the more I realized that we need to look beyond the technical side.

### You Need to Understand the Whole Picture

To improve reliability, we need to understand the product, the business, the people, and how different teams work together.

SRE is connected to almost every technical team, and sometimes even non-technical ones. We can't do our job well if we only understand our own systems. We need to understand how the whole organization works and how its different parts affect reliability.

In practice, that means we should:

1. Look at the bigger picture
2. Document what we learn
3. Analyze the problems
4. Figure out what the next best action is

> **You can't improve what you don't understand, and understanding the whole system means understanding the people and processes behind it, too.**

### Sometimes, It's Just About Talking to the Right Person

When we talk about reliability, we often think about technical solutions, automation, and better tools. But sometimes, the next best action is much simpler.

It might be:

- creating a group chat to connect with another team or a third-party provider
- talking to the right person
- clarifying responsibilities
- making sure important information reaches the people who need it

These things might not look like engineering work, but they can make a real difference.

> **Sometimes, the problem isn't that we need a better technical solution. We just need better communication or a connection that was missing.**

That's why understanding the problem before jumping into a solution is so important.

### We're Building a Culture, Not Just a Technical Foundation

As the first SRE team, we're not just building systems and implementing practices. We're also helping establish how reliability is understood and approached across the organization.

That's not something we can achieve with tools and dashboards alone. We need to:

- share knowledge
- document things
- communicate with other teams
- help make reliability part of how we work together

The technical foundation matters, but the culture around it matters just as much. Otherwise, we might build good tools and processes without creating the environment needed to make them effective.

### What I'm Learning Along the Way

One thing I've learned this year is that being good at SRE requires a lot of people skills and soft skills. It's about talking to people, listening, sharing knowledge, documenting, understanding different perspectives, and working with teams to find the best way forward.

We're not just engineers responsible for keeping services up. We're also trying to understand the whole picture and help the organization make better decisions about reliability.

I'm still learning, and there's a lot more to figure out. But one thing has become clear to me:

> **SRE is not just about making systems reliable. It's about building the technical foundations, relationships, and culture that help an entire organization deliver reliable products.**
